# 0022 — Battle maps are `ArenaGeometry`; Soldiers live on Walk Surfaces and never jump

## Status

Accepted (2026-11-04, `docs/plans/battle-objectives.md`). Stage 3a built the first part: a Battle's map is either the open field or a hand-built `ArenaGeometry` with Battle helpers, the Fort on one level, Soldiers pushed out of its walls. Stage 3b made Squads move well among the walls (Soldiers walk round them, Formations narrow through gates). Stage 4a put Soldiers on several levels (the Walk Surface rule below), built and tested on hand-made maps (a platform with ramps; a rampart with a bridge over a tunnel); Stage 4b grew the Fort a keep with B on its deck, two ramps and a tunnel under it, on those rules.

## Context

A Battle is fought on the Battlefield (ADR 0020): an open heightfield built from its seed, whose triangles are its Walk Surfaces, with no Walls and no Jump Links, and whose height the Crowd reads in constant time. We want maps with walls, gates, breaches, and later raised platforms, ramps, towers and tunnels, fought over by the Squads, Objectives and Commanders of ADR 0021. Every peer must still reach the same state and roll back up to 15 Ticks, and the Crowd must stay about 1 ms a Tick. The ways to give a Battle walls:

- **Walls on the heightfield**: segments stood on the open field. Cheap, but one level only, nothing for an artist to model, and the Fighters' navmesh would be the field's thousands of triangles.
- **A grid of the Crowd's own**: blocked cells the Crowd steers round. Cheap for the Crowd, but the full Fighters stand on Walk Surfaces and stop at Walls (ADR 0006), so there would be two descriptions of one map.
- **`ArenaGeometry` from a Blender generator** (ADR 0006): the map's floor as Walk Surfaces and its walls as Walls, read by the Fighters, the AI's navmesh and the camera as any Arena is, with the Battle's helpers beside them.

## Decision

- **A Battle's map is one of two kinds**: the open field, `battle:<seed>` (the heightfield, unchanged), or a hand-built Battle map, `battle:<map>`, Tuning Data in `data/battlemaps/<map>.json` (`battle.maps`, in the Tuning Data hash), exported by `art/build_battle_<map>.py` from its Collision helpers: the floor (`WALK_*`, convex Walk Surfaces cut along every block's sides), the solid blocks of its walls and towers (`BLOCK_*`, boxes from the ground to a top: the Simulation stands a Wall on each side), other Walls (`WALL_*`, the field's edge), the Objectives (`OBJ_*`, with weight, Owner and spawn), the Bases (`BASE_T*`), the deployment (`DEPLOY`, the front rank's distance from the centre) and the map's army size (a standard and a most). The Battlefield of a map is an ordinary `ArenaGeometry`, cached per Battle Tuning Data like the open field.
- **Full Fighters get the real navmesh, without Jump Links** (`ArenaGeometry.jumpLinks: false`): a 200 m map builds in milliseconds, and promoted AI never leaps off a wall. The AI routes over it as in any Arena; it walks straight only on the open field.
- **The ground is the highest Walk Surface under a point** (`battlefieldHeight` reads the map's Walk Surfaces through its `Heightfield.walk`), so everything that stands on the Battlefield (Soldiers drawn, Promotion, Objective markers) stands on the floor the Fighters do. One level only in Stage 3a.
- **The destination fields go round the walls**: a cell whose centre a block covers is blocked; a blocked cell holds one step more than its lowest open neighbour, so a Squad pushed against a wall still has a way out. A Squad's anchor walks straight at its place only when the line keeps clear of every block (by a Formation's spacing), and is in contact with an enemy only when no block stands between them.
- **Soldiers are pushed out of the blocks** (`pushOutOfBlocks`): a step that walked in from outside slides out by the side it came in through (Stage 3b), else out by the shallowest side that does not land in a touching block. Anchors and respawned Soldiers are too.
- **Soldiers walk round the walls to their places** (Stage 3b): a place inside a wall, or past one from the anchor, moves in toward the anchor (`formationSpot`); a Soldier that cannot walk straight to its place (a block across the line, or within its body of it) walks to the furthest point it can of the line from its anchor out to its place, and when it can reach none of it, down the destination field that leads past its anchor (`toward`), never at a cell centre on a wall's face.
- **Formations narrow to the clearance ahead** (Stage 3b): each field cell stores its clearance (static, beside the fields); a Squad's Formation narrows to as many files as the least clearance from its anchor to `squad.narrow.ahead` past it holds, `squad.narrow.room` from the walls each side, so a line becomes a column through a gate. The narrowing is a function of the anchor's state, worked out each Tick, never stored. A Squad's place at an Objective is one with room for a column, from its own share of a spiral over the circle (mirrored for Team 1).
- **The map is its own half turn**, as the armies are: Objective k mirrors Objective n - 1 - k and block k mirrors block n - 1 - k (and its Walk Surfaces and Walls are each other's half turn as sets), and every tie a Team breaks on the map (the way down a field, the way out of a block) Team 1 breaks the half-turned way, so neither army is favoured by the order of a list.
- **`armySize` and Tickets per map**: the Lobby's map picker folds the map into the Battle's Tuning Data (`applyBattleKnobs`), the army size its standard unless the Lobby picks another, held to its most, and (when the map file gives one) the Tickets a Soldier starts with (`ticketsPerSoldier`: a map that slows the killing, the Fort, starts with fewer). A Battle refuses an army too big for its map.
- **Soldiers live on Walk Surfaces and never jump** (Stage 4a): each Soldier stores the Walk Surface it stands on (`CrowdState.surface`, an index into the map's `walk`; plain state, hashed, snapshotted, in the resync and checked against the map), and so does each Squad's anchor (`SquadState.surface`); a height is never stored, it follows the surface. On the open field, whose ground is the heightfield, both are -1. A Soldier crosses between surfaces only as the navmesh's walk and drop Links do, by the **walk rule** (`levels.ts`): past its surface's edge it walks onto the highest ground there within `battle.levels.stepUp` of its feet, else drops off an open ledge onto ground at most `battle.levels.drop` below, never across a Wall at its height (from `stepUp` to `battle.levels.height` over its feet), and never up anything higher; what stops it, it slides along (along x, then z: the same for both Teams). A Soldier in a tunnel stays on the tunnel's floor, since the bridge over it is higher than a step. The limits are the Battle's own Tuning Data (`battle.levels`), no more than a Fighter's (`fall.stepUp`, and the deepest drop Link). The walk rule is traced in quarter-metre steps over the line, so no Walk Surface is stepped over.
- **Solid parts of a raised level stand over no ground**: a deck, a rampart or a ramp's wedge is a Walk Surface with no floor under it (the ground is cut out there) and Walls on its faces for the Fighters, so Soldiers keep out of it because no ground is there within a step, not by a block. Blocks stay solid from the ground to their top, as in Stage 3a, and nothing stands on them.
- **Only a map of several levels moves by the walk rule** (`Battlefield.layered`: some Walk Surface not flat at the floor's height). On a one-level map (the Fort before Stage 4b) a Soldier's surface is the floor under it and moves nothing, and the destination fields are the grid they were; on the open field nothing of this runs at all. Whole open-field and Fort Battles hash the same as before Stage 4a (the new surfaces left out), with and without a Player.
- **The destination fields have a node per level** on a map of several levels: a cell where levels overlap (a bridge over a tunnel) holds one node for each level standing at its centre, and a node is joined to a neighbouring cell's only where a Soldier walks from one centre to the other by the walk rule (a drop joins one way; a diagonal only where both its straight parts go), so a Dijkstra backward over the joins gives each level its own distances and the anchor its own way down. A point's node is the one of its cell on its Walk Surface's level. A node's clearance is its distance to the nearest block and to the nearest edge of its level a Soldier cannot step off without dropping (a ledge, the foot of higher ground, a Wall, the field's edge), so a Formation narrows on a bridge and in a tunnel as in a gate. A Squad walks straight at its place only where a Soldier walks the line without dropping and arrives on the place's level; its places and its spot at an Objective are ones it walks to on its level (never off a deck's edge), and it is in contact only with enemies of its own level.
- **Soldiers of different levels do not touch**: on a map of several levels a Soldier pushes apart from, sees and fights only Soldiers whose feet are less than `battle.levels.height` from its own, keeps out of the reach only of a full Fighter of its level, and counts on an Objective only on the Objective's level.
- **The Bubble across levels**: Promotion still goes by the distance across the ground, but on a Battle map a Soldier more than `battle.levels.promote` (2.5 m) above or below the Player's Fighter is never promoted (nobody is promoted through a floor), nor waits on its ring on a map of several levels; Demotion keeps its distance and stands the Soldier on the Walk Surface under its Fighter's feet. The open field, one heightfield, has no floors and keeps its Bubble as it was.
- **A map is refused unless every Walk Surface is joined to every other by walking and dropping** (the build-time walk check, `walkCheck`, run when its Battlefield is built; like ADR 0014's judge): each edge of each Walk Surface is probed by the walk rule, and every surface must be reached from the first and reach it back.

## Consequences

- Nothing new enters `SimState` for a map but each Soldier's and each anchor's Walk Surface (Stage 4a, about 430 numbers at 200 v 200): its floor, blocks, fields and navmesh are static caches, built once per map and Tuning Data. Peers on different maps or map files refuse each other through the Tuning Data hash, as with any Arena.
- On a map of several levels the Crowd step traces each Soldier's step over the Walk Surfaces when it leaves its own (most steps stay on it: one polygon test) and reads each Soldier's height from its surface (a plane) for the level tests; Stage 4a's Result has the cost. Building the fields traces every node's eight joins once.
- A Duel's, a Run's and the open field's Battles are unchanged: the open field has no blocks, so its push, its fields and its lines of sight do nothing new, and its AI still never builds a navmesh.
- What the Simulation sees is simpler than what is drawn: rubble in the breaches and the gate arches are render only, and a ruined tower's stump is as solid as a standing one.
- Stage 3a's crude push kept Soldiers out of the walls but not in good order (Soldiers pressed against a wall for minutes); Stage 3b's Result measures how long Soldiers stay out of place with the walking round, the narrowing and the roomy places. Gates stay chokepoints: Squads queue through them, narrowed to one to three files.
- A new map needs its generator to emit the helpers, and the Lobby a name for it (`BATTLE_MAPS`).

## The Fort's keep (Stage 4b)

The first map of several levels that anyone plays. Its generator (`art/build_battle_fort.py`) builds the keep as the test maps do: a deck 4 m up (nine Walk Surfaces, cut along the tunnel's and the ramps' sides), two ramps (sloped Walk Surfaces, 11 m wide, 9 m long, up the west and east faces), no ground under any of them (the ground is cut out under the keep's two halves and the ramps), Walls on the keep's faces, a parapet on its deck's edge, rails on the ramps' sides and the tunnel's sides, and **never a block under a deck**. The tunnel is the ground between the keep's halves, 6 m wide with the deck as its roof. B stands on the deck (an Objective's level is the highest Walk Surface at its centre), so it counts the Soldiers on the keep only, and the Squads' places at B are on the deck. The fields' destination grid is 4 m, so the generator keeps every Wall and block edge off a cell's centre (a node centred on a Walk Surface's edge broke the half turn of the fields) and the ramps' rails off a cell's edge.

Two rules the keep needed beyond Stage 4a's, both only on a map of several levels: a body that stands across a thin Wall from the node the field leads it to (a rail through its cell) takes the node of the cells round it that it can walk to with the least way left (`levelDescend`), and sight over a Wall at the lower body's height is no sight (`inSight` with the two heights), so a Squad is not in contact with an enemy it can only see over a rail and does not walk into the rail at it. Without them whole Battles stalled with both armies pinned to the ramps' rails.

## The Ziggurat (Stage 5)

The second hand-built map of several levels, built wholly on the rules above with no new Simulation rule but one: **an Objective may name its level** (`y` in the map file, `ObjectiveSite.y`). A hand-built map's Objective stands on the highest Walk Surface at its centre (the Fort's B on the keep's deck), which cannot say "the hall floor under the roof". With `y` it stands on the floor there nearest under that height (`groundNear`), so the Ziggurat's B (the plaza floor, `y` 0) and C (the summit, `y` 16) share an x and z and are told apart by level, as Soldiers of different levels already are (they neither see, push nor count for each other's Objective). Absent, nothing changes; the Fort's file is untouched. The Objective marks are drawn flat at the named level.

The temple is a four-tier stepped pyramid (96 m at its foot, a terrace 12 m wide round each tier, floors at 4, 8, 12 and the summit at 16 m: every step above `battle.levels.drop`, so nobody drops off a terrace) on rolling hills, built as the Fort's keep is: raised Walk Surfaces with no ground under them, Walls on every face and a rail on every edge, **no block anywhere**. Its generator (`art/build_battle_ziggurat.py`) reads everything off one raster of 3 m cells (`LEVELS`: per height, the cells with a floor there), so a wall, a doorway or a balcony is whatever the raster leaves open; a cell's top is the temple's top there, and the hollows (the plaza hall under the third tier, six corridors, the balconies) are floors under that roof. Corridors and ramps are 6 m; a ramp is 4 m up in 9 m. Four straight ramps climb each face to the summit, each on the terrace in front of its tier (cut into a tier they would cut the summit into pieces). Inside, a 6 m corridor through each face leads to the plaza; the north and south walls carry a ramp up to a corridor that opens on floor 1's terrace, the east and west walls a long ramp up to a doorway on floor 2's. Objectives: A and D at opposite corners of floor 2, B in the plaza, C on the summit. The hills are 10 m triangles of Walk Surfaces beyond a flat 60 m apron, never under 0 and flat along each army's avenue and at each Base; the trees are render only.

The map is its own half turn (every Walk Surface and Wall has its half turn among them; the plan's Objective k mirrors n - 1 - k holds in x and z, and not in level for B and C, so the two Teams meet a Commander's ties in a different order there: the one asymmetry the map's brief demands).


## The Cavern (lava)

The fourth hand-built map, a sci-fi cave of cube platforms, ramps and bridges over a river of lava, 150 a side (`art/build_battle_cavern.py`). The river is a Walk Surface marked `lava`, 8 m under every walkable floor, so it is deadly ground for full Fighters (a Fighter that steps or is knocked off a bridge or bank dies there) while Soldiers never reach it: no ground lies within `battle.levels.drop` of any ledge over it, so they slide along an open ledge as along a wall (a Soldier that dies in the lava is not simulated; they simply never enter it). Two rules came with it: `walkCheck` leaves deadly surfaces out (they are never walked to or from), and a map's floor, where Players spawn, is its lowest ground that is not deadly. The generator shifts its 4 m plan by half a metre so that no Wall of it lies on a centre of the 5 m destination fields (Squads pinned against rails otherwise).
